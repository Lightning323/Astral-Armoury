## Roadmap
A few changes are needed to change Minecraft's core design philosophy of "items should be impermanent"
1. Removal of anvil "too expensive" cap
   2. An upper limit of XP for expensive items (configurable)
3. Netherite tier or higher of items don't lose durability
   4. An alternative would be that high tier items, when dropped below 2%, stop losing durability and stop working, requiring repairing
6. New soulbound enchantment, when applied the item cannot be dropped upon death
   7. A rebalancing would be to take XP to bring the item back, if the player has no XP, the item is left behind (configurable)
   8. having an XP cost to items just to keep them makes the player think twice on which items they want the enchantment applied to, so its not applied to everything
     

##  True Weapon Variety & Reach Mechanics
Mojang actually prototyped this in Jeb's unreleased Combat Tests (Combat Snapshots):

1. Spears/Tridents: Long reach, narrow hitboxes, thrust attacks. 
2. Axes: Shield-crushing heavy hits that break guards, but slow recovery. 
3. Shortswords/Daggers: Fast attack speed, lower range, higher crit potential on flanking attacks. 
4. Sweeping Swords: Wide crowd-control swings for swarms of light mobs

Very powerful weapons could be powerful to use but at the cost of requiring more skill to use


Powerful ranged weapons:

1. The Greatbow / Siege Bow (Heavy Impact & Precision)
   - The Power: Hits like a truck. It pierces through multiple enemies, knocks heavy mobs back, and deals massive damage capable of chunking bosses or armored targets.
   - The Skill Floor (Drawbacks):
     - Anchored Aim: Pulling the bow completely grounds you or heavily reduces movement speed to a slow crawl.
     - Timing Window (Sweet Spot): Instead of just holding the pull forever, the bow has a "Perfect Draw" timing frame (e.g., a 0.3-second window right when fully drawn). Releasing within this window triggers maximum damage and armor penetration. Releasing too early causes a misfire; holding it too long drains stamina/hunger and shakes your crosshair.
     - Heavy Projectiles: Requires heavy iron/netherite-tipped Greatarrows that have steeper ballistic arcs, requiring players to master long-range trajectory calculations.

2. The Arbalest with Manual Reload (Rhythm & Stance)
- The Power: Fires high-velocity bolts with flat trajectories, high critical multiplier, and built-in shield-bypassing capabilities.
- The Skill Floor (Drawbacks):
  - Active Reload Mechanic: Instead of a passive wait time, reloading requires a active timing minigame (similar to Gears of War active reload or Darkest Dungeon rhythms). Hitting a precise zone on a quick timing bar reloads it rapidly; missing it jams the crank mechanism for a few seconds.
  - Stance Requirement: To fire with full accuracy, the player must be stationary or crouching. Firing while jumping or running causes extreme shot spread.

3. The Staff of Channeling / Alchemy Cannon (Elemental Skillshots)
- The Power: Fires condensed elemental charges (e.g., concentrated dragon-breath plasma, chain-lightning, or kinetic shockwaves) that cause area-of-effect damage or status effects.
- The Skill Floor (Drawbacks):
  - Leading & Travel Time: The projectile moves slowly, requiring players to anticipate enemy movement and "lead" their shots accurately over distance.
  - Overheat / Resource Drain: Rapid firing builds up an "Instability" bar. If overused, it backfires, dealing self-damage or inflicting negative status effects on the wielder.
    Skillshot Detonation: The player must press right-click a second time mid-flight to manually detonate the projectile right next to targets for maximum splash damage (similar to a air-burst detonation).
